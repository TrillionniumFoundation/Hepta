# Cold journal forward-copy prefix doubling

The AZ02 decoder still constructs every logical byte and keeps the original grammar, admission, chunk bounds and byte-equality checks. For a positive exact backward distance and a fully bounded destination, it copies at most the initial distance from the preceding period. It then copies at most the already written prefix on each step. Each source ends at or before its destination, so every bulk move is nonoverlapping and equivalent to the original forward byte propagation. The final copy may be shorter than the current prefix.

Zero-size calls remain zero-write. A future source, mismatched or zero distance, or any out-of-bounds/overflowing extent follows the entire unchanged original fallback, including its word-before-byte ordering, partial writes and trap. The native producer and archive share the same decoder text. This does not introduce a seed profile, new opcode, evidence cache, result reuse or budget change.

The original 3,250 bounded codec cases pass for both ordinary and bulk helpers. The final bulk profile also passes the original full names journal case (59,855 chunks and 692,299,755 logical bytes), bringing its exact original gate count to 3,251. The additional native differential observes all workspace bytes, guards, offset aliases, poisoned future bytes and the original signals/partial writes. The original 18 driver and both AZ01/AZ02 19 archive gates pass. The independently native-built assembly/projector/bulk-seed/packer products match the tested products exactly.

Actual base, chain and multi plans retained all nodes and produced the same public bytes as the prior actual run. Their full archive contents were checked against the live files. This is component performance evidence with historical independent reference inputs, not a fresh full cold replay. The helper speedup did not provide sufficient whole-phase savings.
